Rental-Ready · PM take-home · Tyler Aston · August 2026

Neighbor already refers away the bigger half of the transaction.

Neighbor and RVshare have a live partnership — and it runs in exactly one direction. RVshare sends its owners to Neighbor to find storage. Neighbor sends nobody back, and captures none of the rental economics on a vehicle parked on its own network.

The brief: help renters who store RVs and boats on Neighbor also rent those vehicles out — wireframe the touchpoints across the journey that maximize adoption, and prototype at least one of them. The prototype in section 3 is fully clickable.

Thesis

Three structural advantages no rental marketplace can copy.

Peer-to-peer RV and boat rental is a solved demand problem and an unsolved logistics problem. Neighbor happens to own the three hardest pieces already — because of the storage relationship, not despite it.

Neighbor owns the depot.

The unsolved problem in peer-to-peer RV rental is physical handoff: where does a stranger pick up a 32-foot Class A? Neighbor is the only company with a distributed network of vetted physical locations already co-located with the idle asset. The storage space is the rental depot. Outdoorsy cannot build this without becoming Neighbor.

Neighbor owns the data.

Vehicle class, length, photos, exact location, verified ID and days-parked are all captured during storage onboarding — Neighbor's RV search already asks for class and length up front. A rental listing that takes ~20 minutes to build on RVshare can arrive ~85% pre-filled and be confirmed in 90 seconds.

Neighbor sends the bill.

Reservations are month-to-month, so every storage renter gets a recurring charge for a vehicle that isn't moving. The most persuasive instant to pitch "make this earn" is the instant someone is billed for its idleness. Neighbor owns that moment. Nobody else is in the room for it.

Sell the offset, not the income.

✕  Don't ship

"Earn money renting your RV"

Income framing. Competes head-on with Outdoorsy and RVshare on their turf, and implies effort, an unbounded goal, and a second job. The mental comparison becomes "is this worth becoming an RV-rental operator?" — a high bar most owners will decline.

✓  Ship this

"Cancel your storage bill"

Offset framing, native to a relationship Neighbor already has — it's a direct descendant of "Cancel any month." The goal is small, concrete and finite: $185 — one booking clears it. The comparison becomes "do I want this bill to go away?" Loss aversion does the work, and it yields a retention metric no competitor can even report.

This launch makes Neighbor a three-sided marketplace: storage host, vehicle owner, vehicle renter. The storage host has strangers arriving at their property — so the host holds veto power, and must be compensated for it. Designing only the vehicle owner's flow produces a feature that cannot launch. Host consent and a paid "Handoff Host" tier are load-bearing, and they ship first (screen 6 in the prototype).

The RVshare partnership stays — and becomes bidirectional. Today RVshare's Owner Toolkit sends its owners to Neighbor for storage. Rental-Ready closes the loop: every RVshare-referred RV parked on a consenting lot is day-one rental supply, and every vehicle activated on Neighbor is a storage retention lock. Extend the flywheel, don't kill it.

Metrics & rollout

One booking is worth a year of storage margin.

Storage is a great wedge and a thin margin. Rental on the same asset, on the same lot, is the same relationship at ~12× the take per transaction — and it makes the storage side stickier, not weaker.

Phase 1 · Q4 '26

Phoenix pilot

One RV-dense metro. ~200 stored Class B/C vehicles, concierge onboarding, manual vehicle vetting, Neighbor staff shadowing the first 50 handoffs.

Gate: ≥5% activation · <1% disputes
Phase 2 · H1 '27

RVshare cohort

Make the partnership bidirectional: RVshare-referred vehicles arrive rental-eligible, and "store it free" co-marketing turns their owner base into instant supply.

Gate: host opt-in ≥30% in pilot metros
Phase 3 · H2 '27

GA + boats + depot hubs

Boats as the long tail. Commercial-lot operators — the Fifth Wall / Acadia / Jamestown side of the network — become staffed depot hubs with fleet tooling.

Gate: unit economics ≥ pilot at 10× volume
Risks

What could kill it, and what we'd do about it.

RiskWhy it's realMitigation
Insurance coverageA stored vehicle and a rented vehicle are different underwriting problems; the $1M host protection doesn't cover a renter driving away.Bolt-on rental coverage via a specialty carrier, priced into the renter's service fee — the RVshare/Outdoorsy model. Rental stays off until coverage binds.
Host disruption & churnStrangers on the lot is exactly what some storage hosts signed up to avoid. Silent resentment becomes supply churn.Consent-first (screen 6), per-request veto, frequency caps, and real pay — the Handoff Host tier. Watch the opt-out guardrail weekly.
RVshare relationshipNeighbor's rental side competes with the partner that currently sends it storage demand.Frame as bidirectional flywheel and bring them into Phase 2 economics before GA. The renegotiation window is a date on the calendar, not a surprise.
First-time-lister qualityStored vehicles skew idle; idle vehicles skew unmaintained. A bad first trip burns the renter and the lot.Eligibility gates (model year, days since last movement, photo review), a pre-rental inspection add-on Handoff Hosts can perform — for a fee.

Assumptions to validate in week one

Five things this design assumes that I'd pressure-test with internal data before writing a line of code: (1) current storage protection is bolt-on-extendable for rental rather than needing a new carrier; (2) identity verification depth today (ID scan on file?) — determines whether rental eligibility is one click or one flow; (3) whether storage hosts can already see what's parked on their lot in real time — shapes the consent UX; (4) the 20% take-rate model against Neighbor's actual cost per handoff; (5) whether commercial-lot operators want fleet tooling now or in Phase 3.
